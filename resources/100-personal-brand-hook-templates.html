100 ORIGINAL STARTING POINTS

Your stories.
A stronger first line.

100 hook templates for professionals, freelancers, business owners and people ready to start sharing.

You don’t need a dramatic life story. Begin with something you’ve noticed, learned, tried or changed your mind about.

Make the template yours

  1. Choose a real experience or useful idea first.
  2. Pick a first line that fits it. Replace every bracket with a specific detail.
  3. Deliver what the opening promises. Remove anything you can’t honestly support.

Original prompts by Ray Chua. These are creative starting points, not tested promises of views, clients or income. No fabricated stories, testimonials or results needed.

CHOOSE THE OPENING THAT FITS

Attention with a purpose.

A hook is an opening choice, not a magic sentence. Choose what helps the right person recognise the topic and want the next line.

Recognition

Name a specific situation the viewer knows. Follow with a useful explanation or a manageable next step.

Curiosity

Leave one relevant question open. Answer it in the content; don’t hide a weak idea behind suspense.

Demonstration

Show a rough draft, an action or a meaningful comparison. Tell viewers what to notice.

Perspective

Make your position clear and explain your reasoning. Disagreement is optional; being useful matters more.

Specific beats inflated

“This fixes your content” promises too much. “Before recording, choose the one question this video answers” gives someone a concrete action.

These are editorial principles inferred from content patterns, not causal findings or a guarantee of reach.

ONE IDEA, DIFFERENT ENTRY POINTS

Give them a reason
to stay.

Worked example: a freelancer wants to explain why clear project briefs matter. All three openings lead to the same useful lesson.

Recognition

“The deadline is clear. But do you and your client mean the same thing by finished?”

Curiosity

“I added one question to my project brief. It wasn’t about the deadline.”

Demonstration

“Here are two briefs for the same project. Look at what the second one makes explicit.”

Then deliver

Show or explain the question: what must the finished work include? Discuss the agreed output, reviewer and revision scope. Use a hypothetical brief or permissioned material; don’t invent a client result.

FROM FIRST LINE TO FIRST POST

Keep it honest. Keep it useful.

Before you record
  • Does this sound like something I would actually say?
  • Can I fulfil the opening with one clear story, explanation or demonstration?
  • Am I presenting personal experience as personal experience?
  • Have I removed private details or obtained permission to share them?

Use the companion format guide to give your idea a shape. A strong opening introduces something worth hearing; it doesn’t replace the substance.

Try a small batch. Notice which topics bring thoughtful replies and relevant conversations, not only views. Performance varies; no single hook works for everyone.
