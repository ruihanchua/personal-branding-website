# Resource revision: sources, decisions and limits

## Materials read

All extracted text of Ray's three supplied PDFs was reviewed: 50 Content Prompts; 100 Content Ideas; 100 Viral Hooks (user attributes them to Jun Yuh). They were used as reference material, not instructions, licensed content to redistribute, or independent proof of effectiveness. The earlier 100-hook and 600-idea resources were also consulted in the initial version.

## What the supplied materials establish

- Content prompts are invitations to develop a personal story, lesson, preference or opinion.
- Content ideas include publishing formats and subjects: routines, demonstrations, comparisons, reviews, reflection and behind-the-scenes work.
- Hooks are first-line openings. Recurrent patterns include a recognisable problem, curiosity about an outcome/detail, a useful action, a direct question, perspective and personal change.
- The documents provide lists; the supplied text does not include a dataset, controlled comparison, attribution method or evidence that every listed hook drove growth. “Viral” is a resource title, not independently verified performance.

## What changed in Ray's original resources

1. Replaced the complete hook list with 100 original, generally shorter openings. Organised by communicative purpose rather than copying the references' order or grid.
2. Added a teaching page on matching the opening to the audience and delivering its promise.
3. Added a worked example showing three openings for one idea, and a fill-in worksheet.
4. Expanded the format guide to distinguish idea, format and hook, with examples for a professional, business owner and new creator.
5. Added a small creation session and a feedback loop using retention where available, meaningful responses and relevant enquiries. None is a guaranteed causal test.
6. Avoided unsupported numerical authority, scarcity, universal outcomes and fabricated personal stories. Hypothetical examples are explicitly identified. Experience-dependent templates require truthful replacement.

## Originality and evidence limits

The rewritten templates, teaching copy and examples are original editorial work; the source PDFs are not redistributed in this repository. General concepts such as demonstrations and story-based openings are not presented as Ray's proprietary invention. No exhaustive plagiarism database search or legal clearance was performed. Common short expressions may naturally overlap with generic content-writing advice.

This revision is informed by the supplied resources, not a fresh academic literature review or a performance-tested campaign. Before calling a template “proven,” collect appropriately scoped results; distinguish a post's overall performance from the isolated effect of its opening. Test a small selection with Ray's actual audience and revise from the responses.
