# Ray Chua: landing page and funnel strategy

Prepared 14 October 2026. This replaces the earlier assumption that a 50-hook PDF should be the main offer. The current website is a visual prototype; its hook download is not the approved funnel.

## Goal and audience

Build a personal-branding business with two buying intentions:

- Learn: aspiring creators, working professionals, individuals and business owners who want coaching or teaching to build their own brand.
- Delegate: individuals and businesses who want Ray to handle agreed parts of execution.

Segment by desired help, rather than by job title. Someone can be a business owner and still want to learn. Geography, budget, exact service deliverables, Ray's demonstrated results and delivery capacity are not established.

Success is qualified enquiries and eventual customers. Email subscribers are an intermediate outcome. A large list of freebie seekers is not sufficient evidence that the funnel supports monetisation.

## What the reference funnels actually offer

### Content Academy: educational registration

The primary CTA is register for a free live masterclass. It advertises a creator-career outcome, reduced daily effort, instructor results, student examples, three learning phases, FAQs and event logistics. The hook-writing tool is a bonus for attendance; it is not the primary standalone hook-bank offer. The inspected page describes immediate email delivery of the Zoom link and calendar invitation and scheduled reminders. We did not register to verify fulfilment.

Purpose of the sequence: outcome establishes relevance; instructor/student evidence supports credibility; the syllabus makes value concrete; FAQs address eligibility, time, cost and logistics; repeated CTAs give an action after those questions have been answered. Actual event timing can motivate attendance. Ray should only use deadlines for a real scheduled event.

Transferable: specific learning outcome, a useful syllabus, an opportunity to experience the teacher, clear signup expectations. Not transferable without evidence: large follower/income claims or student results. A live webinar requires preparation, attendance and delivery capacity; it is not automatically better than a recorded lesson.

### Ken Tjandra: identity and coaching application

The primary CTA is apply for coaching. The audience is explicitly faith-based creators and entrepreneurs. A personal timeline establishes identity and affinity. Claimed student wins, problem/fix comparisons and a detailed six-month coaching offer follow. Delivery includes curriculum, group coaching, community and one-to-one support. The business tier for execution is described as available inside the program. FAQs explain fit, commitment, differentiation and price in broad terms; a discovery call/application is the next step.

This is not principally a free-download funnel. It demonstrates a sharply defined audience and a differentiated story-driven approach. Ray can borrow that specificity without borrowing the identity, promises, duration, curriculum or delivery commitments.

### ViralVista: service enquiry

The offer is done-for-you video production. The page reduces uncertainty by explaining onboarding, scripting/filming/editing and revisions, then presents client testimonials. The enquiry CTA offers a 30-minute strategy session with content analysis and a proposed way forward. Its visible form requests name, Instagram handle, phone, email and business context.

A service buyer needs to know what is delegated, what they must do, what happens next and whether the provider can deliver. This is a closer reference for Ray's delegation audience than an educational freebie. Its form length and revision promises are not automatically appropriate for Ray.

### Jonathan Xu: portfolio

Work, visual storytelling, biography and contact demonstrate creative fit. Useful as a design/personality reference, not a direct model of a creator-coaching acquisition funnel.

## Recommended architecture

Use the Instagram bio page as a brief personal-brand hub with two deliberate routes. Each destination then has one main action. The hub can offer two paths because it routes different visitor intentions; the dedicated destination should not require that choice again.

Instagram content → Ray's bio hub → learn route OR delegate route.

Learn route → free short lesson signup → lesson delivery → optional coaching interest → fit conversation → paid coaching/classes.

Delegate route → service explanation and real examples → short enquiry → fit conversation → scoped proposal → engagement.

These follow-up stages are proposed. No email integration, coaching offer, booking system or sales process has yet been configured. Learning visitors can later enquire about execution, and service visitors can watch the lesson; maintain a clear main action per destination.

## Offer recommendation

The strongest starting hypothesis for the learning audience is a short recorded teaching session with a narrow, immediately useful outcome. Provisional concept: “What to post when you want to be known for something.” Work through one brand angle, three content ideas, and one example of adapting a content format. The visitor experiences Ray's thinking and teaching, which is relevant to buying coaching. Ray must record and approve this lesson before advertising immediate access.

A 50-hook bank is quick to consume but can attract people who want only swipe copy. It also does little to demonstrate personalised strategy. Use it as a bonus or an Instagram campaign-specific offer rather than the universal site promise. Viral-format breakdowns are another suitable teaching format if Ray can provide original, well-supported analysis. Views alone are not evidence that a format caused a result.

For delegation, use a concrete service promise and a fit enquiry. Provisional concept: “Build your personal brand with help on the strategy and execution.” Do not promise filming, editing, posting, lead generation or unlimited revisions until Ray confirms those deliverables. A free consultation should say what will be discussed; do not promise a full free strategy plan for every lead without delivery capacity.

## Page sequence and purpose

### Bio hub

1. Recognition: Ray's name/photo and a clear personal-branding promise matching the Instagram content.
2. Relevance: one short statement for people building a brand from their expertise or business.
3. Routing: “I want to learn” and “I want hands-off help,” with concrete explanations.
4. Credibility: original work, a specific teaching example and a short relevant personal introduction.
5. Final routing actions. Keep unrelated external navigation secondary.

### Learning page

1. Headline: name the useful outcome of the free session. CTA: get access.
2. Frustration: describe uncertainty about angle, ideas and creating useful content without overstating pain.
3. Mechanism: preview Ray's approach with a real example, not a vague growth promise.
4. Learning outcomes: three things the visitor will be able to work on after watching.
5. Credibility: relevant experience, original content or genuine student feedback when available.
6. Signup: email; first name optional. Explain delivery and any follow-up emails. Separate a request for the lesson from optional marketing consent where needed. No phone number or survey required for the free lesson.
7. FAQs: beginner suitability, time, price (free), delivery, and what happens next.
8. Repeat the same signup CTA after meaningful decision sections. Avoid several identical buttons without new reasons to act.

### Delegation page

1. Specific outcome and whose problem it solves. CTA: enquire.
2. Work examples: show quality and the reasoning behind decisions; label self-initiated examples as such.
3. Scope: what Ray does, what the client supplies and what is outside the engagement.
4. Process: discovery, agreed plan, execution and review, with details confirmed by Ray.
5. Genuine proof and a relevant introduction.
6. Short form: name, email, profile/business URL optional, and main goal. Ask additional qualification questions later unless needed to route the enquiry.
7. FAQs: fit, client involvement, timing and pricing approach once established.

## Testing plan

Track each source and route separately using campaign parameters and anonymous event counts once a supported analytics tool is selected. Define a qualified enquiry before launch, including service fit, a real need, plausible timing and willingness to discuss scope.

Learning metrics: unique visits, form starts, successful delivered signups, lesson engagement where measurable, coaching enquiries, attended calls and customers.

Delegation metrics: unique visits, enquiry completion, qualified enquiries, attended calls, proposals and customers.

Never log form contents or email addresses into general analytics. Confirm consent and delivery. A click is not a submitted form; a submitted form is not a delivered lesson; a registration is not a customer.

First compare campaign/message fit and actual lead quality. Test the educational offer against a hooks campaign only when both resources exist and traffic is sufficiently comparable. Keep traffic source, audience, device mix and period in view. At low traffic, use visitor feedback and lead conversations rather than claiming statistical certainty from a handful of signups. Do not compare different dates or audiences as if offer alone caused the difference.
